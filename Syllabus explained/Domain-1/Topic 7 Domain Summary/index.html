Domain 1 Summary

The Master Synthesis

A consolidated review of DNA structure, replication physics, and bioinformatics pipelines.

1. Replication (The Copy Machine)

The replisome functions as a high-fidelity biological machine dedicated to duplicating the genome:

  • Helicase & Primase: Unwind DNA and lay down RNA primers to begin synthesis.
  • Polymerase: Synthesizes the new strand strictly in the 5' → 3' direction.

This mechanical process sets the baseline constraints for how genetic information is copied.

2. Fidelity Safeguards

Copying billions of bases without errors requires sequential layers of defense to prevent disease:

  • Base Selection: The initial sorting of nucleotides.
  • Proofreading: Exonuclease activity acts as a backspace key.
  • Mismatch Repair: Post-replication machinery that fixes remaining errors.

Together, these systems reduce the error rate from 1 in 10⁴ to 1 in 10⁹.

3. Okazaki Fragments

Because DNA is antiparallel and polymerase is directional, the Lagging Strand cannot be synthesized continuously:

  • Fragmented Synthesis: Created in short bursts called Okazaki fragments.
  • Ligation: DNA ligase must seal the nicks between fragments to create a continuous strand.

This mechanical constraint is precisely why genome assembly algorithms must handle staggered, fragmented sequencing reads.

4. Thermodynamics of Fidelity

Chemical attraction alone is inherently limited when selecting the correct nucleotide:

  • Thermodynamic Limit: Hydrogen bonding only provides a 1 in 10⁴ accuracy rate.
  • The Free Energy Delta (\( \Delta G \)): The difference between a correct and incorrect match is not large enough to prevent all mutations.

This proves that chemistry requires a biological gatekeeper to achieve high fidelity.

5. Physics of Base Pairing

The extreme stability and specificity of DNA stems directly from physical chemistry:

  • Hydrogen Bonding: Provides the structural specificity (A-T, C-G).
  • Pi-Stacking & Desolvation: Provide the massive thermodynamic stability needed to keep the helix intact.

These mathematically quantifiable forces create the baseline requirements for accessing genetic data.

6. Conformational Entropy

The ultimate kinetic filter utilized by DNA polymerase to block mismatched bases:

  • Active-Site Geometry: Enforces a rigid 10.8 Å width.
  • The Kinetic Barrier: Mismatches clash sterically, causing an insurmountable spike in activation energy (\( \Delta G^\ddagger \)).

This conformational check stalls failing polymerases, bridging passive thermodynamics with active repair.

7. The Bioinformatics Payoff

Bioinformatics is the application of these physical limits into computational algorithms. Because we understand the exact mechanics of replication and error, we build statistical models to filter sequencing data:

Base Quality Score Recalibration

Uses empirical error models (like Naive Bayes in BQSR) to mathematically recalculate Phred quality scores based on specific geometric weak points like dinucleotide context.

Artifact Filtering

Because polymerase physically slips in homopolymer runs, PairHMM algorithms dynamically slash gap open and extend penalties to penalize known mechanical stutter artifacts.

Mutational Signatures

Because polymerases have specific geometric biases (e.g., preferentially swapping C for T), broken mismatch repair pathways leave distinct mathematical patterns in tumor genomes. Recognizing these molecular "fingerprints" allows us to trace evolutionary history and predict immunotherapy response.
