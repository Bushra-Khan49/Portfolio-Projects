Domain 1 Summary

The Master Synthesis

A consolidated review of DNA structure, replication physics, and bioinformatics pipelines.

1. The Structural Foundation

DNA is the ultimate archival database of life (Topic 0). Its extreme stability stems directly from physical chemistry (Topic 5):

  • Hydrogen Bonding: Provides base-pairing specificity.
  • Pi-Stacking & Desolvation: Provide massive thermodynamic stability.

These chemical forces are mathematically quantifiable (\( \Delta G \)), creating the baseline requirements for accessing and copying genetic data.

2. The Copy Machine & Discontinuous Replication

The replisome functions as a high-fidelity biological machine (Topic 1). Because DNA is antiparallel and Polymerase synthesizes strictly 5' → 3', the Lagging Strand must be synthesized discontinuously (Topic 3).

  • RNA primer deposition initiates synthesis.
  • Elongation forms Okazaki fragments.
  • Primer removal and nick sealing by DNA ligase complete the strand.

This mechanical constraint is precisely why genome assembly algorithms must handle staggered, fragmented reads.

3. The Three-Tiered Fidelity System

Copying billions of bases without errors requires three sequential layers of defense (Topic 2, 4, 6):

1. Thermodynamic Attraction

Baseline specificity based on hydrogen bonding. Yields a 1 in 10⁴ error rate.

2. Kinetic Gatekeeper

Enforces rigid 10.8 Å active-site geometry. Stalls mismatched bases via \( \Delta G^\ddagger \) spikes, yielding 1 in 10⁷ accuracy.

3. Mismatch Repair

Post-replication proofreading machinery. Brings final accuracy to 1 in 10⁹.

The polymerase acts as the ultimate gatekeeper, bridging the gap between passive thermodynamics and active mismatch repair.

4. The Bioinformatics Payoff

Bioinformatics is the application of these physical limits into computational algorithms. Because we understand the exact mechanics of replication and error, we build statistical models to filter sequencing data:

Base Quality Score Recalibration

Uses empirical error models (like Naive Bayes in BQSR) to mathematically recalculate Phred quality scores based on specific geometric weak points like dinucleotide context.

Artifact Filtering

Because polymerase physically slips in homopolymer runs, PairHMM algorithms dynamically slash gap open and extend penalties to penalize known mechanical stutter artifacts.

Mutational Signatures

Because polymerases have specific geometric biases (e.g., preferentially swapping C for T), broken mismatch repair pathways leave distinct mathematical patterns in tumor genomes. Recognizing these molecular "fingerprints" allows us to trace evolutionary history and predict immunotherapy response.
