TOPIC 12

EVOLUTIONARY LOGIC

From Molecular Damage to Variant Calling

Why Are We Starting This?

In Topic 11, we saw how DNA uses Thymine to flag chemical damage (like cytosine deamination) so repair enzymes can fix it.

But what happens when that damage escapes repair and undergoes replication? A transient chemical lesion becomes a permanent genomic mutation.

We are starting this topic because the sequencer does not see "damage"—it only sees abstract letters. To understand bioinformatics, we must understand how a molecular accident transforms into a statistical variant call.

Where Does This Fit?

This is the bridge between physical reality and computational data. It fits exactly at the boundary where molecular biology ends and data science begins:

1. Chemistry (Spontaneous Deamination) ↓ 2. Biology (Failed Repair & Replication) ↓ 3. Technology (Sequencing the Mutation) ↓ 4. Computation (Variant Calling)

Understanding this pipeline prevents the most dangerous assumption in bioinformatics: mistaking a computational observation for a biological truth.

Damage vs. Mutation

This distinction is fundamental.

Chemical damage: \( C \rightarrow U \)
This is a lesion. The original genomic information has not necessarily been permanently changed yet. If repaired, no mutation remains.

But if replication converts the lesion into a stable base-pair change:

\( C:G \rightarrow T:A \)

then we have a mutation.

\( \boxed{ \text{damage} \neq \text{mutation} } \)

Mutation can be the heritable consequence of unrepaired damage.

Transitions vs. Transversions

When damage becomes a mutation, it falls into one of two classes.

Transitions (Like replaces Like)
Purine → purine: \( A \leftrightarrow G \)
Pyrimidine → pyrimidine: \( C \leftrightarrow T \)

Transversions (Category Swap)
Purine ↔ pyrimidine: \( A/G \leftrightarrow C/T \)

Because cytosine (a pyrimidine) deaminates to uracil (another pyrimidine), the resulting mutation is a transition:

\( \boxed{C\rightarrow T = \text{transition}} \)

The Epigenetic Trap: 5-Methylcytosine

We established that DNA uses Thymine so that when Cytosine deaminates into Uracil, the repair machinery knows U doesn't belong.

But there is a major complication: Epigenetics. DNA often contains 5-methylcytosine (5mC), which is cytosine with a methyl group added to silence gene expression.

If 5-methylcytosine undergoes deamination, the chemistry changes dramatically:

\( \boxed{5mC \xrightarrow{\text{deamination}} T} \)

The resulting base (T) is a normal DNA base. It creates a T:G mismatch that is incredibly difficult for the cell to distinguish from a normal T. The error signal is completely lost.

\( \boxed{ 5mC\text{ deamination evades the simple “U is abnormal” strategy} } \)

This is why methylated regions (like CpG islands in vertebrate genomes) are massive hotspots for C→T mutations. The cell's security system is blind to them.

Sequencing Does Not See "C"

Once a mutation occurs, we sequence the genome. But this introduces a massive conceptual gap.

A FASTQ file contains ACGTACCGT...

But the sequencer did not directly see a letter C. It measured a physical signal—like fluorescence flashes, electrical current disruptions, or hydrogen ion releases.

The computational system transforms this:

\( \boxed{\text{physical signal}} \rightarrow \boxed{\text{base call}} \)

Even the letter "C" in a FASTQ file is an abstraction. It is an inference made by an algorithm, susceptible to noise.

Three Levels of the Same Event

This brings us to one of the most useful mental models in bioinformatics.

Level 1 — Molecular
\( C:G \rightarrow U:G \)
A physical chemical damage event.

Level 2 — Genetic
\( C:G \rightarrow T:A \)
A stable, heritable sequence change.

Level 3 — Computational
REF=C, ALT=T
An observation reported by a Variant Caller.

The VCF does not know the molecular mechanism. It only knows the statistical observation.

What Does a Variant Caller Do?

Suppose the reference genome has C. At one position, the sequencer provides 100 reads. 80 say C, and 20 say T.

A naive allele fraction is:

\( \boxed{AF = 20\%} \)

But the caller cannot simply say "20% T means a mutation." It must consider sequencing errors (the noise in the physical signal). If the sequencer's error rate is 0.1%, it expects some fake Ts.

A real variant must generate substantially more T observations than expected from random sequencing noise.

The Bayesian View

Conceptually, the caller calculates the probability that a variant actually exists given the reads it sees: \( P(\text{variant}\mid\text{reads}) \).

Using Bayes’ theorem:

\( \boxed{ P(V|D) = \frac{P(D|V)P(V)}{P(D)} } \)

The key term is \( P(D|V) \) vs \( P(D|\neg V) \):

\( \boxed{ \frac{P(D|V)}{P(D|\neg V)} } \)

It asks: How much more strongly do these reads support a real mutation rather than just sequencing noise?

Mutation Spectra

Once we can call variants reliably, we don't just look at one. We look at millions. If we simply record C→T, we discard vital information.

Instead, we record the sequence context: 5'-A[C>T]G-3'.

By classifying all mutations by their context, we build a mutation spectrum.

\( \boxed{\text{millions of variants}} \rightarrow \boxed{\text{structured mutation profile}} \)

If a specific profile is heavily enriched for C→T in CpG contexts, we can mathematically link the computational data all the way back to 5-methylcytosine deamination.

Reverse-Complement Normalization

DNA is double-stranded. If one strand contains a C→T mutation, the complementary strand inherently contains a G→A mutation.

They are the exact same physical event viewed from opposite sides.

To avoid double-counting in mutation spectra, computational analyses choose a canonical representation (usually pyrimidines).

Thus, every G→A is computationally flipped and represented as its complement C→T.

This is a beautiful example of molecular structure directly dictating computational data design.

The Traps of Computation

When we see REF=C, ALT=T in a VCF, it carries a massive stack of hidden assumptions:

  • The reads were generated without chemical degradation in the library.
  • The physical signals were called accurately by the sequencer.
  • The read aligned to the correct genomic coordinate (no mapping artifacts).
  • The read pileup represents independent physical molecules, not just PCR duplicates.

Suppose PCR amplification creates 20 copies from a single damaged DNA molecule. The sequencer outputs 20 reads showing T. It looks like overwhelming statistical evidence. But it is 20 reads representing 1 original molecule.

If we treat read count as independent evidence, the statistics fail. The computational system must constantly fight to reconstruct reality.

The Pipeline as a Chain of Filters

Every stage in bioinformatics is a filter. If an upstream stage is fundamentally wrong (e.g., chemical damage in the sample), the downstream algorithms can execute perfectly and still produce a completely false biological conclusion.

Structure (The Methyl Group) ↓ Chemistry (Deamination) ↓ Damage (C→U) ↓ Repair (UNG Enzyme) ↓ Replication (U:A → T:A) ↓ Mutation (Fixed C→T) ↓ Measurement (Fluorescent Signals) ↓ Statistics (Bayesian Inference) ↓ Variant (VCF File) ↓ Biological Inference

Understanding the methyl group on thymine is not a small biological detail. It is the perfect example of how molecular chemistry ultimately becomes computational data.
